# AI and GenAI Fundamentals

Artificial Intelligence is the broad field of building systems that perform tasks requiring human-like intelligence: reasoning, classification, planning, perception, language understanding, and decision-making. Machine Learning is a subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data. Generative AI is a subset focused on creating new content such as text, images, audio, code, and video.

Modern GenAI interviews usually test whether you can build useful systems on top of foundation models, not whether you can train a frontier model from scratch. You should be able to explain model capabilities, limitations, cost, latency, data privacy, hallucination, evaluation, and how the AI feature fits into a real product workflow.

## Interview Questions & Answers

**Q: What is Generative AI?**  
A: Generative AI refers to AI systems that create new content based on patterns learned from training data. Examples include text generation, image generation, code generation, summarization, translation, and conversational assistants.

**Q: How is GenAI different from traditional ML?**  
A: Traditional ML often predicts labels, scores, or classes for a specific task. GenAI produces new outputs and is usually powered by foundation models that can perform many tasks through prompting, adaptation, retrieval, or tool use.

**Q: What is a foundation model?**  
A: A large model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many downstream tasks. LLMs are foundation models for language, while multimodal models can process text, images, audio, or video.

**Q: What should a production GenAI engineer know beyond prompting?**  
A: RAG, evaluation, safety, latency, cost, observability, data privacy, fallback behavior, prompt/version management, and integration with application systems.
