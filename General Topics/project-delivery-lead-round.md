# Project Delivery Lead Round

Project delivery lead interviews test whether you can deliver outcomes through planning, execution, risk management, stakeholder communication, team coordination, and technical judgment.

## Core Responsibilities to Discuss

- Sprint planning and backlog refinement
- Estimation and capacity planning
- Risk and dependency tracking
- Client or stakeholder communication
- Team coordination
- Release planning
- Quality assurance
- Production readiness
- Incident management
- Delivery metrics
- Technical debt management

## Common Questions and Strong Answer Direction

**Q: How do you plan a project from scratch?**  
A: Clarify scope, stakeholders, success criteria, timeline, risks, team capacity, architecture needs, milestones, QA strategy, release plan, and communication cadence.

**Q: How do you handle scope creep?**  
A: Document new requirements, assess impact, explain tradeoffs, reprioritize with stakeholders, and avoid silently absorbing extra work.

**Q: How do you estimate work?**  
A: Break work into smaller tasks, include unknowns, dependencies, QA, review, deployment, and buffer for risk. Use historical velocity when available.

**Q: How do you track delivery health?**  
A: Sprint progress, blockers, defect leakage, cycle time, burn-down, deployment frequency, incident count, and stakeholder feedback.

**Q: How do you manage risks?**  
A: Maintain a risk register, assign owners, define mitigation plans, review risks regularly, and escalate early.

**Q: How do you handle team conflicts?**  
A: Listen individually, align on facts, bring focus back to project goals, define working agreements, and follow up.

## Delivery Lead Interview Stories to Prepare

- Project delivered under pressure
- Production issue handled successfully
- Difficult stakeholder managed
- Scope change negotiated
- Team conflict resolved
- Junior developer mentored
- Technical debt reduced
- Release planned and executed

## Common Pitfalls

- Talking only about task tracking, not outcomes.
- Ignoring quality and production readiness.
- Blaming team members for delivery problems.
- Not showing stakeholder communication.
- Missing risk management examples.
