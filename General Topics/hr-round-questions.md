# HR Round Questions

HR rounds usually test communication, stability, motivation, culture fit, compensation expectations, notice period, and whether your career story makes sense. The goal is not to sound perfect; the goal is to sound clear, honest, professional, and reliable.

## Common Questions and Strong Answer Direction

**Q: Tell me about yourself.**  
A: Give a 60-90 second summary: current role, years of experience, primary technologies, type of projects handled, leadership or ownership areas, and what you are looking for next.

Example: "I am a full-stack engineer with experience building MERN/MEAN applications, cloud deployments, APIs, and team delivery. Recently I have focused on architecture, scalable backend services, performance improvements, and mentoring developers. I am now looking for a role where I can contribute at a senior technical level and also take ownership of delivery and design decisions."

**Q: Why are you looking for a change?**  
A: Keep it positive. Mention growth, broader responsibility, architecture ownership, cloud exposure, leadership, or product impact.

**Q: Why should we hire you?**  
A: Connect your skills to their need: technical depth, delivery ownership, stakeholder communication, mentoring, and production problem solving.

**Q: What are your strengths?**  
A: Pick strengths with proof: ownership, debugging, system design, communication, mentoring, learning speed, or delivery discipline.

**Q: What is your weakness?**  
A: Choose a real but controlled weakness and show improvement. Avoid fake answers like "I work too hard."

**Q: Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?**  
A: For senior roles, say you want to grow as a technical leader: architecture, delivery, mentoring, scalable systems, and business-aligned engineering decisions.

**Q: Why this company?**  
A: Mention product domain, engineering maturity, scale, cloud/platform work, learning opportunity, and alignment with your next career step.

**Q: Are you comfortable working with cross-functional teams?**  
A: Yes, and give examples: product managers, QA, DevOps, designers, clients, business stakeholders.

**Q: How do you handle pressure?**  
A: Talk about prioritization, communication, breaking problems down, escalation, and keeping stakeholders informed.

**Q: Describe a conflict with a colleague.**  
A: Use STAR. Focus on listening, facts, alignment, and outcome. Do not blame.

## HR Topics to Prepare

- Career summary
- Reason for job change
- Current responsibilities
- Team size and reporting structure
- Notice period
- Current CTC and expected CTC
- Work location and relocation preference
- Shift flexibility
- Client-facing experience
- Leadership and mentoring experience
- Long-term career goal

## Common Pitfalls

- Speaking negatively about previous employers.
- Giving unclear salary expectations.
- Overexplaining personal reasons.
- Claiming leadership without examples.
- Sounding desperate instead of purposeful.
